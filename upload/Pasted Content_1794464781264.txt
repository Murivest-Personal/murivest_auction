The objective is to build a premium, search-engine-friendly Kenyan property auction marketplace that generates qualified purchaser inquiries and supports Murivest's 2% buyer-paid brokerage business.

4. Important implementation decisions
There are four things I would insist the coding AI gets right.

1. Protect your sourcing relationships without compromising disclosures.

Create two separate sets of fields in Sanity:

publicInformation: property information that visitors can see.

internalInformation: source identity, private contacts, negotiations, internal documents and confidential notes.

The public API must use explicit field projections. Hiding fields in the frontend is insufficient.

2. Do not confuse auction inquiries with formal bids.

I recommend initially making the website an auction discovery and buyer acquisition platform. Buyers register interest and receive assistance. Formal bids go through the legally authorised auction channel unless Murivest has an authorised arrangement to receive them.

3. Make the 2% fee a proper commercial process.

A buyer should understand and accept the brokerage agreement before Murivest undertakes the agreed representation services. Record the agreement and its acceptance in Supabase. Keep the brokerage fee separate from auction deposits.

4. Make SQL generation dependent on actual database inspection.

The AI should inspect existing tables and policies before generating migrations. Otherwise, you risk duplicate tables, conflicting policies and breaking existing applications.
